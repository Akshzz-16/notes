# Unit 1: Organizational and Individual Behaviour

## 1.1 Definition, Scope and Importance of OB

### Definition of OB
Organizational Behaviour (OB) is the systematic study and application of knowledge about how individuals and groups act within organizations. It examines the impact of structure, culture, and processes on behaviour to improve organizational effectiveness.

### Scope of OB
- **Individual Level**: Personality, perception, attitudes, motivation, learning
- **Group Level**: Team dynamics, leadership, communication, conflict
- **Organizational Level**: Culture, structure, change, power & politics

### Importance of OB
- Improves interpersonal relationships
- Enhances employee performance and productivity
- Helps predict and control human behaviour
- Improves job satisfaction and reduces turnover
- Facilitates effective leadership and communication
- Promotes innovation and organizational change

### Fundamental Concepts of OB

| Concept | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Perception** | Process of interpreting sensory information to understand the environment |
| **Attitude** | Learned predisposition to respond consistently in a favourable/unfavourable manner |
| **Personality** | Unique set of enduring traits and characteristics that influence behaviour |
| **Learning** | Relatively permanent change in behaviour due to experience |
| **Motivation** | Internal drive that directs behaviour toward goals |

### Relationship Between OB and the Individual
- OB helps understand why individuals behave differently in similar situations
- Individual behaviour is shaped by both internal factors (personality, values) and external factors (organizational culture, rewards)
- OB provides frameworks to align individual goals with organizational goals
- Organizational effectiveness depends on how well individual behaviour is managed and directed

## 1.2 Models of OB

### 1. Autocratic Model
- **Basis**: Power and authority
- **Managerial Orientation**: Command and control
- **Employee Orientation**: Obedience and dependence
- **Employee Outcome**: Minimal performance, compliance
- **Psychological Result**: Frustration, dependency on boss

### 2. Custodial Model
- **Basis**: Economic resources and benefits
- **Managerial Orientation**: Money and security
- **Employee Orientation**: Passive cooperation
- **Employee Outcome**: Satisfaction but not strong motivation
- **Psychological Result**: Dependence on organization, not fully engaged

### 3. Supportive Model
- **Basis**: Leadership and support
- **Managerial Orientation**: Coaching and facilitating
- **Employee Orientation**: Job performance and participation
- **Employee Outcome**: Moderate engagement, initiative
- **Psychological Result**: Feeling of belonging and contribution

### 4. Collegial Model
- **Basis**: Partnership and teamwork
- **Managerial Orientation**: Collaboration and shared responsibility
- **Employee Orientation**: Self-discipline, responsibility
- **Employee Outcome**: High involvement, ownership
- **Psychological Result**: Self-actualization, sense of contribution

### 5. SOBC Model (Stimulus-Organism-Behaviour-Consequence)
- **Stimulus (S)**: External triggers in the work environment
- **Organism (O)**: Internal cognitive and affective processes of the individual
- **Behaviour (B)**: Observable actions and responses
- **Consequence (C)**: Outcomes and reinforcements that follow the behaviour
- Focuses on how environmental stimuli influence internal states, which drive behaviour and its consequences

### Model Comparison

| Model | Basis | Focus | Employee Response |
|-------|-------|-------|-------------------|
| Autocratic | Power | Authority | Obedience |
| Custodial | Economic resources | Security | Passive cooperation |
| Supportive | Leadership | Support | Participation |
| Collegial | Partnership | Teamwork | Self-discipline |
| SOBC | Stimulus-response | Behavioural analysis | Adaptive behaviour |

## 1.3 Individual Behaviour

### Definition
Individual behaviour refers to the actions, reactions, and conduct of a person in a workplace setting. It encompasses how an individual performs tasks, interacts with colleagues, and responds to the organizational environment.

### Importance of the Individual
- Every individual brings unique skills, perspectives, and experiences
- Individual performance aggregates to organizational performance
- Understanding individual differences helps in effective job design, training, and leadership
- Employee satisfaction and retention depend on how individuals are treated

### Key Factors Influencing Behaviour

#### 1. Personal Factors
| Factor | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Personality** | Stable traits that determine consistent patterns of behaviour |
| **Values** | Deeply held beliefs about what is right/wrong or important |
| **Attitudes** | Evaluative judgments about people, objects, or situations |
| **Abilities** | Innate and acquired skills that affect performance |
| **Background** | Family, education, culture, life experiences |

#### 2. Environmental Factors
| Factor | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Culture** | Shared values, norms, and beliefs of the organization |
| **Leadership** | Style and quality of supervision and management |
| **Work Environment** | Physical conditions, resources, ergonomics |
| **Team Dynamics** | Peer pressure, group norms, social support |
| **Organizational Structure** | Hierarchy, reporting relationships, policies |

#### 3. Psychological Factors
| Factor | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Motivation** | Internal drive that energizes and directs behaviour |
| **Emotional Intelligence** | Ability to recognize, understand, and manage emotions |
| **Cognitive Biases** | Systematic patterns of deviation from rational judgment |
| **Perception** | How individuals interpret and make sense of their environment |
| **Learning Style** | Preferred way of acquiring and processing information |

## 1.4 Personality

### Meaning of Personality
Personality is the unique and relatively stable pattern of thoughts, feelings, and behaviours that distinguishes one person from another. It is shaped by both genetic (nature) and environmental (nurture) factors.

### Theories of Personality

#### 1. Trait Theory
- Assumes personality is composed of a set of stable and enduring characteristics (traits)
- Traits are consistent across situations and over time
- Key contributors: Gordon Allport (cardinal, central, secondary traits), Raymond Cattell (16 PF), Hans Eysenck (PEN model)

**Allport's Trait Levels**:
- **Cardinal Traits**: Dominant traits that define a person's entire life (rare)
- **Central Traits**: Major characteristics that form the foundation of personality (5-10 per person)
- **Secondary Traits**: Situational preferences and attitudes

**Eysenck's PEN Model**:
- **Psychoticism**: Aggressive, egocentric, antisocial vs. empathetic, cooperative
- **Extraversion**: Sociable, lively, outgoing vs. reserved, quiet
- **Neuroticism**: Anxious, moody, unstable vs. calm, stable

#### 2. Big Five Traits (OCEAN)
The most widely accepted model of personality in OB:

| Trait | Description | High Score | Low Score |
|-------|-------------|------------|-----------|
| **Openness** | Willingness to try new experiences | Creative, curious, open-minded | Conventional, prefers routine |
| **Conscientiousness** | Degree of organization and dependability | Organized, reliable, disciplined | Disorganized, careless, impulsive |
| **Extraversion** | Sociability and energy level | Outgoing, talkative, assertive | Reserved, solitary, quiet |
| **Agreeableness** | Tendency to be cooperative and compassionate | Cooperative, trusting, kind | Competitive, sceptical, critical |
| **Neuroticism** | Emotional instability and negative affect | Anxious, moody, insecure | Calm, stable, resilient |

### Effects of Personality on Work Behaviour

| Personality Factor | Effect on Work |
|-------------------|----------------|
| **Conscientiousness** | Strongest predictor of job performance across all occupations |
| **Extraversion** | Better performance in sales and management roles |
| **Emotional Stability** | Higher job satisfaction, lower burnout |
| **Agreeableness** | Better team collaboration, service orientation |
| **Openness** | Higher creativity, adaptability to change |

### Personality and Job Satisfaction
- **Locus of Control**: Internal locus leads to higher satisfaction (belief that one controls outcomes)
- **Self-Efficacy**: Higher confidence leads to greater perseverance and success
- **Self-Esteem**: Higher self-esteem correlates with job satisfaction
- **Type A/Type B**: Type A (ambitious, impatient) more prone to stress; Type B (relaxed, patient) higher satisfaction

## 1.5 Perception

### Meaning and Concept of Perception
Perception is the cognitive process through which individuals select, organize, and interpret sensory information to make sense of their environment. It is the lens through which reality is filtered and understood.

**Key points**:
- Perception is subjective — different people perceive the same situation differently
- Perception shapes behaviour, not objective reality
- Perception is influenced by characteristics of the perceiver, the target, and the situation

### Factors Influencing Perception

#### 1. Perceiver Factors
- **Attitudes, motives, and interests**
- **Past experiences and expectations**
- **Personality and values**
- **Current emotional state**

#### 2. Target Factors
- **Physical appearance and attractiveness**
- **Size, intensity, and contrast**
- **Novelty and familiarity**
- **Movement and sound**

#### 3. Situational Factors
- **Physical setting** (lighting, noise, layout)
- **Social context** (presence of others, norms)
- **Organizational environment** (culture, rules, timing)

### Perceptual Process

```
           INPUT                    PROCESS                    OUTPUT
    ┌──────────────┐         ┌─────────────────┐         ┌──────────────┐
    │ Environmental │  ──→   │  Selection of   │  ──→   │  Organized   │
    │  Stimuli      │         │  Stimuli        │         │  Perception  │
    └──────────────┘         └─────────────────┘         └──────┬───────┘
                                                                │
                                                                ▼
    ┌──────────────┐         ┌─────────────────┐         ┌──────────────┐
    │   Behaviour  │  ←──   │  Interpretation  │  ←──   │   Meaning    │
    │   Response   │         │  & Evaluation    │         │  Assignment  │
    └──────────────┘         └─────────────────┘         └──────────────┘
```

**Steps in Detail**:
1. **Selection**: Filtering stimuli based on attention, interest, and relevance
2. **Organization**: Categorizing and structuring selected information
3. **Interpretation**: Assigning meaning based on past experiences, expectations, and mental models
4. **Response**: Behavioural reaction based on the interpreted perception

### Social Perception

#### Stereotyping
- **Definition**: Attributing generalized characteristics to all members of a group
- **Process**: Categorizing people based on visible traits (age, gender, race, profession)
- **Effects**: Leads to biased judgments, oversimplification, and discrimination
- **Examples**: "Older employees resist change", "Millennials are entitled"
- **Impact on OB**: Affects hiring, promotion, performance appraisal, team dynamics

#### Halo Effect
- **Definition**: Allowing one positive (or negative) trait to influence the overall impression of a person
- **Process**: A single outstanding characteristic overshadows all other traits
- **Examples**:
  - "She's good-looking, so she must be competent"
  - "He was late once, so he must be irresponsible"
- **Impact on OB**: Leads to inaccurate performance evaluations, biased recruitment decisions
- **Avoidance**: Use structured evaluations, focus on multiple specific criteria

### Other Perceptual Biases

| Bias | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Selective Perception** | Seeing only what we want to see |
| **Contrast Effect** | Evaluating based on comparison to others seen recently |
| **Projection** | Attributing one's own feelings to others |
| **Self-Fulfilling Prophecy** | Expectations cause behaviour that confirms the expectation |
| **Fundamental Attribution Error** | Overestimating personal factors, underestimating situational factors |

## Summary

- **OB** is the systematic study of individual and group behaviour in organizations
- **Five models of OB** evolve from autocratic to collegial, with SOBC adding a behavioural framework
- **Individual behaviour** is influenced by personal, environmental, and psychological factors
- **Personality** theories (Trait Theory, Big Five) help predict work performance and satisfaction
- **Perception** is subjective and influenced by perceiver, target, and situational factors
- **Biases** like stereotyping and halo effect can distort judgment and decision-making
