# Unit 2: Business Planning and Strategy

## 2.1 Business Model Canvas

### Concept of a Business Model
A business model describes how an organization **creates, delivers, and captures value** — how it makes money by solving customer problems. It answers: who is the customer, what value do we offer, how do we reach them, and how do we earn revenue.

### Business Model Canvas (BMC)
The Business Model Canvas is a **strategic management tool** developed by **Alexander Osterwalder and Yves Pigneur**. It provides a one-page visual framework of nine building blocks that describe how a business creates and captures value.

### The Nine Building Blocks

| # | Building Block | Definition | Key Questions |
|---|----------------|------------|---------------|
| 1 | **Customer Segments** | The different groups of people/organizations the business aims to reach and serve | Who are our most important customers? |
| 2 | **Value Propositions** | The bundle of products/services that create value for a specific customer segment | What problem do we solve / need do we satisfy? |
| 3 | **Channels** | How a company communicates with and reaches its customer segments to deliver value | Through which channels do customers want to be reached? |
| 4 | **Customer Relationships** | The types of relationships a company establishes with specific segments | What relationship does each segment expect? |
| 5 | **Revenue Streams** | The cash a company generates from each customer segment | What value are customers willing to pay for? |
| 6 | **Key Resources** | The most important assets required to make the model work | What resources does our value proposition require? |
| 7 | **Key Activities** | The most important actions required to make the model work | What activities are essential to deliver value? |
| 8 | **Key Partnerships** | The network of suppliers and partners that make the model work | Who are our key partners and suppliers? |
| 9 | **Cost Structure** | All costs incurred to operate the business model | What are the most important costs in the model? |

### BMC Layout (Left-to-Right Flow)
```
Partners │ Activities │            Value           │ Relationships │ Segments
         │ Resources  │          Proposition       │ Channels      │
─────────┼────────────┼────────────────────────────┼───────────────┼─────────
   Cost Structure              │              Revenue Streams
```

### BMC Example: Food Delivery Startup
| Block | Example |
|-------|---------|
| Customer Segments | Urban professionals, students |
| Value Proposition | Fast, convenient home delivery with live tracking |
| Channels | Mobile app, website, SMS |
| Customer Relationships | Self-service app, loyalty program |
| Revenue Streams | Commission per order, delivery fees, ads |
| Key Resources | App platform, delivery fleet, restaurant network |
| Key Activities | Order processing, delivery logistics, partner management |
| Key Partnerships | Restaurants, payment gateways, courier partners |
| Cost Structure | App development, delivery staff, marketing, cloud hosting |

### Benefits of BMC
- Provides a **single-page overview** of the whole business
- Encourages experimentation and quick iteration
- Highlights **how value flows** and where money comes in/goes out
- Easy to communicate and share with teams and investors

### SPPU Exam Tip
> "Business Model Canvas" is a favourite 7–8 mark question. List the **9 building blocks** with a table, then draw the canvas layout and fill it with one startup example. Marks are awarded for naming all nine blocks correctly.

## 2.2 Creating a Business Plan

### Concept of a Business Plan
A business plan is a **written document** that describes a business, its objectives, strategies, target market, financial forecasts, and the resources needed to succeed. It acts as a roadmap for the business and a communication tool for investors.

### Importance of a Business Plan
- Provides **direction and clarity** to the entrepreneur
- Attracts **investors, lenders, and partners**
- Helps estimate **financial requirements** and feasibility
- Serves as a **benchmark** to measure progress
- Identifies risks and challenges early

### Key Components of a Business Plan

| Component | What It Covers |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Executive Summary** | Snapshot of the entire plan — mission, product, market, finance (written last, read first) |
| **Company Description** | Vision, mission, objectives, legal structure, location |
| **Market Analysis** | Industry trends, target market, customer needs, competitors |
| **Products/Services** | Description, features, benefits, uniqueness, lifecycle |
| **Marketing and Sales Plan** | Pricing, promotion, distribution, sales strategy |
| **Operations Plan** | Production/delivery process, facilities, equipment, suppliers |
| **Management and Organization** | Team, roles, organizational structure, advisors |
| **Financial Plan** | Start-up costs, revenue forecasts, break-even analysis, funding needs |
| **Risk Analysis** | Key risks and mitigation strategies |
| **Appendix** | Resumes, legal documents, supporting data |

### Steps to Prepare a Business Plan
1. Define the vision, mission, and objectives
2. Conduct market research and analysis
3. Define the product/service and its value proposition
4. Develop marketing, sales, and operations strategies
5. Prepare financial projections (startup cost, revenue, profit, break-even)
6. Identify risks and contingency measures
7. Review, refine, and present the final document

### Financial Terms in a Business Plan

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Start-up capital** | Initial money needed to launch the business |
| **Revenue forecast** | Estimated future sales income |
| **Break-even point** | Point where total revenue = total cost (no profit, no loss) |
| **Cash flow** | Movement of money in and out of the business |
| **Gross margin** | Revenue minus cost of goods sold |

### Common Mistakes to Avoid
- Unrealistic financial projections
- Ignoring the competition
- Writing for self instead of the reader (investor)
- Overly long and unfocused content
- Not defining a clear target market

### SPPU Exam Tip
> For "components of a business plan", use the **table of 9–10 components** with one-line descriptions. For a full-mark answer, start with definition + importance, and remember: **executive summary is written last but appears first**.

## 2.3 Strategic Planning for Entrepreneurs

### Concept of Strategic Planning
Strategic planning is the process of defining an organization's **long-term direction** and deciding how to allocate resources to achieve its goals. It bridges the gap between where the business is now and where it wants to be.

### Steps in the Strategic Planning Process

#### 1. Mission, Vision, and Values
- **Vision**: Where the business wants to be in the future (aspiration)
- **Mission**: What the business does, for whom, and why (purpose)
- **Values**: Guiding principles and beliefs

#### 2. Environmental Analysis (External)
- Analysing opportunities and threats in the external environment (PESTEL: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, Legal)

#### 3. Internal Analysis
- Analysing strengths and weaknesses inside the firm (resources, capabilities)

#### 4. Strategy Formulation
- Setting goals and choosing strategies at corporate, business, and functional levels

#### 5. Strategy Implementation
- Converting plans into action — budgets, processes, people, timelines

#### 6. Strategy Evaluation and Control
- Measuring performance against goals and making corrections

### Strategic Planning Levels

| Level | Focus | Example |
|-------|-------|---------|
| **Corporate strategy** | Which businesses/industries to compete in | Diversify into a new market |
| **Business strategy** | How to compete in a specific market | Cost leadership or differentiation |
| **Functional strategy** | How each function supports the business | Marketing, finance, operations plans |

### Tools for Strategic Planning
- **PESTEL analysis** — external environment scanning
- **SWOT analysis** — internal strengths/weaknesses + external opportunities/threats
- **Porter's Five Forces** — industry competitiveness
- **SMART goals** — Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound

### Entrepreneur vs. Traditional Manager in Strategy

| Aspect | Entrepreneur | Traditional Manager |
|--------|--------------|---------------------|
| **Time horizon** | Long-term, growth-oriented | Short-term, operational |
| **Risk approach** | Calculated risk-taking | Risk minimization |
| **Resource use** | Lean, innovative, flexible | Formal, budgeted |
| **Opportunities** | Proactively seeks new ones | Responds to existing plan |

### SPPU Exam Tip
> "Strategic planning" questions: give the **6-step process** with one line each, then mention PESTEL, SWOT, and SMART goals as tools. A 7-mark answer needs the steps table plus one tool explained.

## 2.4 Risk Management and Contingency Planning

### Concept of Risk
Risk is the possibility of loss, damage, or an adverse outcome due to uncertainty. For entrepreneurs, risk is an inherent part of starting and running a business — the skill lies in **identifying, assessing, and managing** it, not avoiding it.

### Types of Business Risk

| Risk Type | Description | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Market risk** | Changes in demand, competition, or customer preferences | New competitor undercuts prices |
| **Financial risk** | Cash flow shortfalls, funding issues, currency changes | Inability to pay suppliers on time |
| **Operational risk** | Failures in processes, people, or systems | Equipment breakdown, staff attrition |
| **Technological risk** | Obsolescence or failure of technology | Core platform becomes outdated |
| **Legal/Regulatory risk** | Changes in laws, compliance, contracts | New tax or data protection rules |
| **Strategic risk** | Wrong decisions about direction or positioning | Entering a declining market |
| **Environmental risk** | Natural disasters, pandemics, supply chain shocks | Flood disrupts warehouse |

### Risk Management Process

1. **Risk identification** — list all potential risks affecting the business
2. **Risk assessment** — evaluate likelihood (probability) and impact (severity)
3. **Risk prioritization** — rank risks using a likelihood-impact matrix
4. **Risk response planning** — decide on mitigation strategies
5. **Risk monitoring and review** — track risks continuously and update plans

### Risk Response Strategies

| Strategy | Approach | When to Use |
|----------|----------|-------------|
| **Avoid** | Eliminate the risk by not doing the activity | High probability + high impact |
| **Reduce (mitigate)** | Lower the probability or impact | Medium/high risk that can be controlled |
| **Transfer** | Shift risk to another party (insurance, contracts) | Financial risk, third parties |
| **Accept** | Acknowledge and absorb the risk | Low probability + low impact |

### Likelihood-Impact Matrix
```
                 Impact
               Low        High
      ┌──────────┬──────────┐
High  │  Monitor │  CRITICAL│
      │  (watch) │ (act now)│
──────┼──────────┼──────────┤
Low   │  Accept  │  Transfer│
      │ (ignore) │ (insure) │
      └──────────┴──────────┘
```

### Contingency Planning
A contingency plan (Plan B) is a **predefined course of action** to be executed if a risk materializes or if the primary plan fails. It ensures the business can respond quickly and minimize disruption.

#### Elements of a Contingency Plan
- **Triggers**: Warning signs that indicate the plan must be activated
- **Response actions**: Specific steps to take when the risk occurs
- **Responsibilities**: Who does what during the crisis
- **Resources**: Backup funds, suppliers, equipment, or personnel
- **Communication**: How stakeholders will be informed

#### Example Contingencies
- Backup supplier if the main supplier fails
- Emergency fund / line of credit for cash shortfalls
- Alternative revenue stream if the core product declines
- Remote-work / backup systems for technology failures

### SPPU Exam Tip
> For risk management: give the **4 response strategies** (avoid, reduce, transfer, accept) as a table, then explain the **5-step risk management process**. A case-based question wants you to *apply* these to the given business scenario.

## 2.5 SWOT Analysis

### Concept of SWOT
SWOT analysis is a strategic planning tool used to evaluate a business's **Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats**. It combines internal analysis (S, W) with external analysis (O, T) to support decision-making.

### The Four Elements

| Element | Internal/External | Positive/Negative | Definition |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------|------------|
| **Strengths** | Internal | Positive | Resources and capabilities that give an advantage |
| **Weaknesses** | Internal | Negative | Limitations that put the business at a disadvantage |
| **Opportunities** | External | Positive | Favorable trends or gaps the business can exploit |
| **Threats** | External | Negative | External factors that could harm the business |

### SWOT Matrix

```
              Helpful             Harmful
              (to objectives)     (to objectives)
Internal      STRENGTHS           WEAKNESSES
External      OPPORTUNITIES       THREATS
```

### Example SWOT: Startup Food Delivery Service
| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|-----------|------------|
| Fast app and easy UX | Limited delivery fleet |
| Strong partner restaurant network | High cash burn, thin margins |
| Loyal early customer base | Small brand awareness |

| Opportunities | Threats |
|---------------|---------|
| Growing urban food delivery demand | Big players (Zomato/Swiggy) |
| Corporate lunch tie-ups | Rising fuel and staff costs |
| Expansion to new cities | Regulatory changes |

### How to Conduct SWOT
1. Identify **strengths** — unique skills, assets, brand, technology
2. Identify **weaknesses** — resource gaps, cost issues, skill shortages
3. Identify **opportunities** — market growth, new technology, policy support
4. Identify **threats** — competition, regulation, economic downturn
5. Cross-match to build strategies (SO, WO, ST, WT)

### Using SWOT to Build Strategies (TOWS)

| Combination | Strategy Focus |
|-------------|----------------|
| **S+O** | Use strengths to capture opportunities (growth) |
| **W+O** | Overcome weaknesses to exploit opportunities |
| **S+T** | Use strengths to defend against threats |
| **W+T** | Minimize weaknesses and avoid threats (survival) |

### Limitations of SWOT
- Can oversimplify complex situations
- Subjective — depends on who fills it in
- Lists factors but does not prioritise them
- Must be updated regularly to stay valid

### SPPU Exam Tip
> SWOT questions: draw the **2x2 matrix** and fill it with the case business's factors. For the highest marks, end with a **TOWS combination** (e.g., "use strength X to grab opportunity Y") — examiners award marks for application.

## Case Study Framework: Business Plan Preparation — Idea to Incubation

SPPU expects students to prepare and discuss a **start-up idea case study** covering the following dimensions:

### 1. Idea Identification
- Source of the idea (personal problem, market gap, trend)
- Which opportunity recognition technique was used (brainstorming, observation, SCAMPER)

### 2. Validation
- Was the problem real and painful for customers?
- Methods used: customer interviews, surveys, MVP, pilot testing
- Proof that customers will pay for the solution

### 3. Incubation
- Support sought: incubators, accelerators, mentors, government schemes
- Resources provided: funding, office space, guidance, networking

### 4. Needs
- The core customer problem or unmet need being solved

### 5. Solutions
- The product/service offered and how it addresses the need

### 6. Target Customers
- Customer segments — demographics, behaviour, geography

### 7. Innovative / Novelty / Unique Features
- What makes the idea different from existing offerings
- Technology, business model, pricing, or experience advantage

### 8. Social Impact
- Job creation, environment, inclusion, community benefit

### 9. Current Status and Discussion
- Stage of the venture (idea, MVP, launched, scaling)
- Challenges faced and lessons learned
- Next steps and future roadmap

### SPPU Exam Tip
> For the unit 2 case study, prepare **one complete business idea** (e.g., a sustainable food-delivery app, an EdTech platform for regional languages) and be ready to answer all **9 dimensions** above. The BMC and SWOT of that idea are very likely to be asked together with the case.

## Unit 2 Quick Revision

- **BMC** = 9 blocks: segments, value proposition, channels, relationships, revenue, resources, activities, partnerships, costs.
- **Business plan** = roadmap + investor pitch; components: executive summary, market analysis, product, marketing, operations, finance, risk.
- **Strategic planning** = vision/mission → analysis → formulation → implementation → control.
- **Risk management** = identify → assess → prioritise → respond → monitor; strategies: avoid, reduce, transfer, accept.
- **Contingency plan** = Plan B with triggers, actions, responsibilities, and backup resources.
- **SWOT** = internal strengths/weaknesses + external opportunities/threats; use TOWS to build strategies.
- **Case study** = idea identification → validation → incubation, covering needs, solution, customers, uniqueness, social impact, and current status.
